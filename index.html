Understanding the Problem

What is E-Waste?

Electronic waste — or e-waste — is any discarded electrical or electronic device. It is one of the fastest-growing waste streams in the world.

What Is E-Waste?

E-waste includes any end-of-life electrical or electronic equipment — from smartphones to refrigerators — that has been discarded or is no longer functional.

Why Is It Increasing?

Rapid technological advancement, shorter product lifecycles, and rising consumer demand lead to more devices being replaced and discarded at an accelerating rate.

Common Examples

Phones, computers, tablets, TVs, printers, batteries, chargers, cameras, gaming consoles, and household appliances are all forms of e-waste.

Why Disposal Matters

E-waste contains valuable recoverable materials and hazardous substances. Proper disposal protects the environment, human health, and recovers reusable resources.

Categories

Types of E-Waste

Different electronic devices require different handling. Understand your e-waste category before disposal.

Mobile Phones

Smartphones and feature phones contain lithium batteries, circuit boards, and toxic metals that require specialized recycling.

Tip: Wipe data, remove the SIM card, and hand it to an authorized mobile recycler.
Laptops & Computers

Computers hold hard drives, RAM, capacitors, and potentially hazardous materials such as lead and mercury.

Tip: Perform a full factory reset and remove storage drives before dropping off.
Televisions

Older CRT and modern flat-panel TVs both contain hazardous components including phosphors, lead, and brominated flame retardants.

Tip: Never smash the screen — take whole units to a registered TV recycling point.
Batteries

Lead-acid, lithium-ion, and nickel-cadmium batteries are highly toxic and can cause fires if improperly disposed of in landfills.

Tip: Tape battery terminals and deposit at a dedicated battery collection bin.
Printers

Printers contain toner cartridges, circuit boards, and plastic components that must be separated and recycled appropriately.

Tip: Remove and recycle toner cartridges separately through manufacturer take-back programmes.
Household Electronics

Microwaves, hair dryers, electric kettles, and similar appliances contain motors, heating elements, and wiring that can be partially recovered.

Tip: Check if any appliance can be repaired before deciding to recycle it.
Consequences

Environmental Pollution

Burning e-waste releases toxic gases including dioxins and furans into the atmosphere, contributing to air pollution and climate change.

Soil Contamination

Heavy metals like lead, cadmium, and mercury leach from improperly landfilled e-waste into surrounding soil, making farmland and water sources unsafe.

Water Pollution

Toxins from e-waste seep into groundwater, rivers, and lakes, harming aquatic life and contaminating drinking water supplies.

Resource Loss

Electronic devices contain valuable recoverable materials — gold, silver, copper, rare earth elements — that are permanently lost when e-waste is not recycled properly.

Health & Safety Risks

Communities near informal e-waste processing sites face serious health risks including respiratory disease, neurological damage, and increased cancer risk.

Global Inequity

Much of the world's e-waste is shipped to developing countries where informal workers, including children, process it in dangerous conditions.

Step-by-Step

Responsible Disposal Guide

Follow these steps to dispose of your electronic devices safely and responsibly.

1
Identify the Item

Determine what type of electronic device you have and understand what materials it may contain.

2
Reuse or Repair?

Check whether the device can be repaired, donated, or sold before considering disposal. Extending device life is the most sustainable option.

3
Remove Personal Data

Perform a factory reset on phones and computers. Use data-wiping software for storage devices to protect your personal information.

4
Separate Batteries

Remove batteries from devices where possible. Batteries must be disposed of separately at designated battery collection points.

5
Find a Facility

Locate an authorized e-waste collection centre or manufacturer take-back programme near you. Use our collection center finder below.

6
Dispose Responsibly

Drop off your e-waste at the authorized facility. Ask for a receipt or confirmation if available, especially for bulk items.

Do
  • Use authorized e-waste collection centers
  • Wipe personal data before handing over devices
  • Separate batteries from the main device
  • Donate working electronics to those in need
  • Ask about manufacturer take-back programmes
  • Repair devices when economically feasible
Don't
  • Throw electronics into regular household waste
  • Burn or incinerate e-waste informally
  • Smash or break devices in an uncontrolled way
  • Leave batteries loose in bins — tape the terminals
  • Pour chemicals from devices down drains
  • Hand devices to unverified/informal collectors

Make a Difference

Small Actions, Big Impact

Every individual choice matters. Here are practical steps you can take today.

Repair Before Replacing

Many electronic issues can be fixed cheaply by a technician. Repairing extends the product's useful life and delays it entering the waste stream.

Donate Usable Electronics

A device that's outdated for you may be valuable to a school, charity, or community centre. Donate before you discard.

Recycle Responsibly

Always use authorized e-waste facilities. Never hand devices to informal collectors or leave them at the side of the road.

Buy Only What You Need

Avoid upgrading devices unnecessarily. Mindful purchasing reduces the total volume of e-waste generated over time.

Never Use General Bins

Electronic devices must never be thrown into regular household waste bins. They require separate, specialist recycling streams.

Protect Personal Data

Always perform a factory reset and use data-wiping software on storage devices before recycling or donating electronics.
